## PGS interpretation of inverted vernacular

PGS currently recognises six candidate inversion classes:

1. Negation inversion — unwanted state used where an affirmative desired state preserves meaning.
2. Agency inversion — material actor/responsibility obscured.
3. Epistemic inversion — interpretation, belief or allegation expressed at an unsupported certainty level.
4. Referential inversion — material actor/object replaced by ambiguous reference or abstraction.
5. Structural inversion — syntax, tense, modifiers or dependencies obscure the underlying proposition.
6. Lexical inversion — proposed morphological/lexical analysis conflicts with conventional surface interpretation.

The first five can contain normative and research subrules. Lexical inversion remains research-led until individual mechanisms are validated.

## Engineering consequence

PGS will not be implemented as a single rewrite prompt. The target pipeline is:

`Formal Engine → Semantic Engine → Fidelity Verifier → Structured PGS Output`

The Formal Engine handles deterministic or explicitly formalised patterns. The Semantic Engine handles contextual meaning and intention. The Fidelity Verifier blocks or flags transformations that materially change propositions, evidence status, protected negation, actor responsibility or requested outcomes.
