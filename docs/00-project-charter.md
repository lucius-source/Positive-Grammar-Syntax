# PGS Project Charter

## Purpose

Positive Grammar Syntax (PGS) defines a trainable and implementable discipline for improving everyday English communication across channels and document types.

PGS seeks to increase shared understanding, explicit agency, factual precision, constructive intent and useful action while reducing avoidable ambiguity, inversion, unnecessary negation, unsupported certainty, passive obscuring of agency and unresolved complaint.

PGS is intended to operate as a ubiquitous language layer rather than a single email template or document style.

## Primary objective

Define the minimum coherent set of language rules that maximises clarity, agency, coherence and constructive intent while preserving meaning and the expressive richness of English.

## Scope

PGS should ultimately support:

- email composition and review;
- email attachments and general documents;
- business and professional writing;
- personal communication;
- chat and messaging;
- web forms and publishing;
- AI prompts and AI-generated text;
- APIs and software integrations;
- education and daily language training;
- future speech/transcription workflows.

## Core analytical model

PGS may analyse communication using five informational components:

1. Actor — who acts or bears responsibility?
2. Observation — what is directly observed, reported or evidenced?
3. Meaning — what interpretation or conclusion is drawn?
4. Intention — what state or outcome is sought?
5. Action — what specific act, request, decision or next step follows?

Not every sentence needs all five components. The model is an analytical framework, not a compulsory sentence template.

## Governing principles

### Agency over obscurity

Where responsibility matters, make the actor visible rather than hiding agency behind passive or abstract construction.

### Observation before assumption

Separate what happened from what the communicator concludes about what happened.

### Desired state over avoidant framing

Where meaning permits, state the wanted state rather than defining communication only through what is unwanted.

### Specificity over avoidable ambiguity

Use concrete actors, objects, conditions, quantities and timeframes when these improve understanding.

### Calibrated certainty

Represent observation, inference, belief, assumption, allegation and established fact at their proper evidential level.

### Intention and action over unresolved complaint

Where the communication seeks change, connect the identified problem to a request, remedy, decision or next action.

### Constructive expression without semantic distortion

Affirmative expression is desirable only where it preserves the proposition. Necessary negation must remain available.

## Semantic fidelity principle

PGS is not a mechanism for making every sentence sound positive. A transformation fails if it materially changes what the communicator means.

Examples of protected negation include:

- `The payment was not received.`
- `The claimant did not serve the notice.`
- `Do not energise the circuit.`
- `This medicine must not be combined with X.`

A PGS transformation may clarify such statements but must not reverse, soften or remove their operative meaning.

## Modes

PGS should support multiple modes because communication improvement does not always require rewriting:

- Analyse
- Score
- Suggest
- Transform
- Compare
- Explain / Teach

## Initial channel

Email is the first operational adapter because it provides frequent, bounded communication units and commonly includes attached documents. The email adapter must use the universal PGS engine rather than define a separate email-only grammar.

## Research provenance

Quantum Grammar Parse Syntax and related language systems may be maintained as research provenance and conceptual inputs. Their propositions should be distinguished from validated linguistic rules, implementation requirements and measurable PGS outcomes.

## Success criteria

PGS succeeds when independent users or implementations can apply the same specification to a communication and obtain substantially consistent findings; transformations preserve material meaning; explanations identify the applicable rules; and improvements remain natural enough for ordinary daily use.
